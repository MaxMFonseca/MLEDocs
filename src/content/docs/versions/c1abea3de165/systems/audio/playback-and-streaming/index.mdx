One-shots and streams begin with the same WAV decoder but retain different resources. A one-shot is decoded and uploaded once into an OpenAL buffer. A stream load keeps decoded float samples in memory and feeds one of eight fixed stream slots through up to four OpenAL buffers of 8,192 samples each. Non-looping streams queue fewer buffers when a short selected duration reaches its end during the initial fills. This is buffered playback from memory, not file-on-demand streaming.

## Load and one-shot path

`Load{name, stream=false}` resolves `res/sounds/<name>.wav`, accepts mono or stereo, uploads float32 sample data, and stores the OpenAL buffer under the hashed name. Reloading replaces and deletes the old buffer. `PlayOneShot` requires that cache entry, a nonzero priority, a valid bus, and an eligible source. It sets pitch, source-relative or world-space state, gain, and non-looping playback.

One-shot `PlayParams.start_offset_ms`, `duration_ms`, `fade_in_ms`, and `fade_out_ms` are declared but not read by `processCmdPlayOneShot`. Do not rely on one-shot seeking, duration windows, or fades at this commit.

## Eight fixed stream slots

`Load{name, stream=true}` retains an immutable `WavData`. `StartStream` selects slot `id` in `0–7`, finds the loaded name, validates bus/volume/pitch and the time window, then stops and replaces that slot. Each active slot owns a shared reference to its decoded data, so reloading the same name does not invalidate playback already in progress.

Offsets and durations are milliseconds. The implementation converts them to interleaved sample indices: stereo advances two samples per frame. `duration_ms = 0` means play from the offset to the end. An offset at or beyond the end, a positive duration that rounds to zero samples, a non-finite volume, or a non-positive/non-finite pitch rejects the start. Streams whose complete decoded sample count is at most one 8,192-sample buffer are also rejected.

Pitch is clamped to `0.01–4.0`; source volume is clamped to `0–4`. Looping wraps inside the selected offset/duration window rather than across the whole file. Non-looping slots clean themselves when OpenAL stops them.

## State changes and ramps

- `PauseStream` pauses an active, unpaused slot. While paused, its volume ramp does not advance.
- `ResumeStream` plays that paused source and resets the ramp tick; it does not explicitly rewind the source.
- `SetStreamParams` can change pitch and/or volume. A positive `fade_ms` replaces the current ramp from its value at command-processing time.
- `StopStream` stops immediately when `fade_out_ms` is zero; otherwise it installs a ramp to zero whose completion stops and clears the slot.
- A ramp carries `RampCompletion::NONE` for an ordinary gain transition or `RampCompletion::STOP` when reaching zero must clear the slot. `advanceRamp` returns a `RampAdvance` containing the new volume and consumes that completion once.
- `StopAll` immediately clears every stream and stops active one-shots. Its declared `fade_out_ms` is ignored by the handler, and it cancels any in-progress stream fade by clearing that slot and ramp.

`StartStreamGroup` is C++-only. It accepts one to eight distinct valid slots, preflights all members, prepares them, then calls `alSourcePlayv` once in source order. A failure clears the touched slots.

## Evidence boundary

The Core suite test-backs sample windows, loop wrapping, ramp replacement, pause-safe ramp arithmetic, pitch validation, retained decoded-data lifetime, group validation, and synchronized call ordering. The Audio Test demonstrates audible pause/resume, fades, duration windows, and looping, but its playtest checklist remains pending rather than an automated audio-output assertion.
